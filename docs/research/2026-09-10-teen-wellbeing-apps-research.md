# Teen & youth-athlete wellbeing apps — a survey of real, named products

**Date:** 2026-09-10
**Scope:** What actually-shipping apps and platforms for 16–18s (and youth athletes) do with their wellbeing check-in — question design, cadence, who sees results, and what the student gets back.
**Builds on:** [`2026-09-09-checkin-safeguarding-research.md`](./2026-09-09-checkin-safeguarding-research.md) (detection accuracy, thresholds, statutory context) and [`2026-09-09-student-app-engagement-research.md`](./2026-09-09-student-app-engagement-research.md) (engagement, reciprocity, notification budget). Neither of those is repeated here; where a named product settles a question those docs left open, it is flagged.

**Current state under comparison:** 5 items (`mood`, `sleep`, `energy`, `stress`, `football_enjoyment`), 1–5 scale, fortnightly Monday push, one question per screen with an optional per-question free-text note, submitted to a single "Thanks for checking in 💙 / Your responses have been saved" screen. Sources: `lib/wellbeing/wellbeingUtils.ts`, `app/(student)/wellbeing/page.tsx`.

## 1. Executive summary

- **The strongest predictor of an honest answer is not anonymity or wording — it is whether the student can see that answering changed something.** Two independent bodies of evidence converge on this. Saw, Main & Gastin's implementation study of athlete self-report measures found athletes stop telling the truth when the loop is invisible: *"if they think that no-one's looking at it then they'll just give dummy responses."* A 2023 qualitative study of 11 world-class team-sport athletes found the same: *"If my information is acted upon then I'll tell the truth"* — and, from an athlete whose data was ignored, *"I'll just give you a different score than I really feel."* **This app is currently the textbook worst case: the free-text note is written to the database and displayed to nobody, and the student's only reward for completing is a thank-you card.** That is not a UX gap, it is a data-validity defect — every recommendation below is downstream of it.
- **The one product that most closely matches this app's situation — youHQ, a UK schools-and-colleges wellbeing platform — solves reciprocity with a single feature this app lacks: "students can review their own trends over time."** It also splits the check-in in two: a low-friction emoji check-in *every time a student logs in*, plus periodic validated instruments (WHO-5, GAD-7, MHC-SF, SDQ). Copying the split is more useful than copying any individual question.
- **Cadence: last night's instinct to increase frequency needs qualifying, and the evidence cuts the other way for the *mandated* survey.** A 2025 government trial across **153 schools and 12,166 pupils** found four of five tested wellbeing interventions produced adverse or negative effects on some pupils, with short-term gains and worse long-term outcomes. Former Ofsted Chief Inspector Amanda Spielman's reading: *"we should not collect data by constantly asking children who are not unwell about their well-being."* STEER Education's own cadence analysis lands on **termly as "about right", daily/weekly as risking fatigue, disengagement and harm, annual as too slow.** The correct move is not "ask more often" — it is **keep the mandated cadence low and add an always-open, student-initiated channel**, which is exactly the shape Kooth and Tellmi take.
- **The `stress` reverse-coding bug identified last night has a clean, evidence-backed escape route: WHO-5.** Five items — same length as the current survey — **all positively worded** (no reverse-coded trap possible), a 0–5 frequency scale, a two-week reference window that maps exactly onto this app's fortnightly cadence, a published screening cut-off (<50/100, sensitivity 89% / specificity 86%), and validated in adolescents. It is free to use and already in production in youHQ.
- **Every UK safeguarding case-management product treats role-based access as the default, not a feature.** CPOMS StudentSafe advertises "intelligent role-based access" letting schools "control who has access to what information and when"; Tes MyConcern's headline is "access all information — confidentially… shared with the right people at the right time." `/admin/wellbeing` currently has no role check at all and reads via the service-role client. This app is the outlier among its own product category.
- **The academy-specific version of the trust problem is deselection fear, and it is well documented.** Players report *"living in fear that any disclosure of personal information will be transmitted to others within the organisation"*, and the in-house-clinician literature identifies the core risk as *"players' fear that their MH disclosures may not remain confidential, potentially affecting team selection."* A check-in whose results are visible to every coach is configured to produce optimistic answers from exactly the students who need help.
- **Finch shows how to get the engagement benefit of a streak without the failure mode teens explicitly rejected:** the user **opts in** to a 2, 5, 7 or 14-day commitment, and missing a day carries no penalty — the app just asks again. That is compatible with last night's finding that teens reject imposed streaks and badges on mood check-ins.
- **Be clear about the evidence boundary: the exact default question sets, scales and alert thresholds of the commercial academy platforms (Kitman Labs, Smartabase/Teamworks, TeamBuildr, Perch, Hudl) are not publicly documented.** What *is* public is the underlying instrument they all customise from — the Hooper index (sleep, stress, fatigue, muscle soreness) — and the published implementation guidance around it. No specific claims about those vendors' question wording appear below.

### 2e. Youth/athlete wellness monitoring — what is public and what is not

**Publicly documented and directly usable:**

- **The Hooper index (Hooper & Mackinnon, 1995)** is the instrument nearly every commercial AMS customises from: **fatigue, stress, muscle soreness, sleep quality**, rated 1–7 where 1 = "very, very low/good" and 7 = "very, very high/bad", summed for an overall wellness score. Note it is **uniformly negatively anchored** — the *whole* scale runs bad-is-high, which is precisely how you avoid the reverse-coding bug this app has: consistency across items matters more than the direction you pick.
- **[AthleteMonitoring](https://www.athletemonitoring.com/wellness-monitoring/)** publicly states its wellness constructs — sleep, stress, fatigue, mood, soreness — completed *"every morning on their smartphone"*, with a coach team dashboard and *"automated alerts… [highlighting] athletes that warrant immediate individual attention."* The alert thresholds are not published.
- **[TeamBuildr AMS](https://www.teambuildr.com/platform-ams)** publicly documents daily check-ins where athletes *"log pain location, rate its severity, and keep you updated on injuries"* via a body heat map. Its wellness-question defaults, customisation options, readiness-score maths and alert logic are **not publicly documented.**
- **Analysis convention (published practitioner guidance, [Global Performance Insights](https://www.globalperformanceinsights.com/post/wellness-questionnaires-for-athlete-monitoring)):** compare each athlete against **their own 28-day rolling baseline using z-scores**, flag beyond **±1.5 SD**, and — the important part — *"significant deviations in wellness metrics should trigger a **conversation** rather than immediate action."* This corroborates the individual-baseline recommendation in last night's doc and adds the specific parameters.
- **Burden matters and is quantified by contrast:** RESTQ-Sport has **76 items** and is explicitly described as *"far too high of a burden to ask of our athletes to complete regularly"* — the reason the field standardised on 4–6 single-item measures. **This app's 5 items are already the right length; the problem is never length.**

**Explicitly NOT publicly documented — do not assume specifics:**

- The default wellness question sets, scale points, or alert thresholds used by **Kitman Labs**, **Smartabase / Teamworks AMS**, **TeamBuildr**, **Perch (Catapult)** or **Hudl**. All are configurable per-client; vendors publish capabilities, not question wording.
- Whether any specific **Premier League or EFL academy** runs a wellbeing check-in resembling this one, what it asks, or how often. The **EPPP** requires clubs to run sessions with contracted players and implement a **Mental and Emotional Wellbeing Action Plan**, and the **PGAAC** (EPL/EFL/FA, 2018) provides independent quality assurance — but the operational detail of individual clubs' check-ins is not public. The one published pilot with detail is a **stepped-care pyramid at Burnley FC Academy (Category 1)**, described below.
- Whether academy wellbeing data is typically visible to coaching staff. The literature discusses this as a *problem*, not as a documented practice.

## 3. Cross-cutting patterns

### What the best examples have in common

1. **The student gets something back, immediately and visibly.** youHQ shows the student their own trend. Daylio returns correlations. Finch's bird grows. Kooth routes a feeling straight to help. Every one closes the loop in the same session.
2. **Two channels, not one.** A cheap, ambient, high-frequency signal (youHQ's login emoji, Kooth's "I'm feeling…") sits alongside a slower, formal, validated measure (WHO-5, GAD-7, termly AS Tracking). Neither channel is asked to do the other's job.
3. **Always-open beats scheduled.** Kooth and Tellmi have no survey cadence at all — the student initiates, 365 days, no referral, no waiting list. The mandated instruments that *do* have a cadence run **termly or annually**, not weekly.
4. **The escalation path has a named owner and a record.** CPOMS and MyConcern both auto-notify the DSL specifically, timestamp everything, support escalation into a case, and build a chronology. Bounce Together routes concerning survey answers straight into that machinery rather than into a staff inbox.
5. **Validated instruments over home-grown items where a defensible measurement is needed.** youHQ and Bounce Together both ship published scales rather than inventing questions. It removes the wording risk, gives you a cut-off, and makes the data defensible to an inspector or a parent.
6. **Positive or consistently-anchored wording.** WHO-5 is entirely positively worded. Hooper is entirely negatively worded. Neither mixes directions inside one scale.

### What the worst examples have in common

1. **Write-only collection.** The failure mode named explicitly in the athlete literature: *"if they think that no-one's looking at it then they'll just give dummy responses."* This app's free-text `note` field is the purest possible instance of it.
2. **High-frequency mandated emotional questioning.** The 153-school / 12,166-pupil trial found four of five interventions had adverse effects on some pupils; Spielman's warning is that children *"start to believe they are sad or worried or afraid, where this has not even occurred to them."* Daily mandated mood checks are the pattern to avoid, not to aspire to.
3. **Imposed streaks and badges on emotional data.** Teens rejected these outright in the qualitative work cited in last night's doc; Finch's opt-in variant is the only version that survives contact with that finding.
4. **Ambiguity about who can see the answers.** In the athlete self-report study, athletes *"guessed or listed only a few of the multiple staff with access"*, and the researchers named it *"an issue of informed consent that needed to be addressed."* Uncertainty alone suppresses honest reporting — you do not need an actual leak.
5. **Directive rather than passive framing.** From the teen research in last night's doc: "you need help" measurably worsens wellbeing where "do you want to chat to someone today?" does not. Kooth's entire entry point is passive and self-selected.

## 4. The trust/authority dynamic — how an academy-mandated check-in must differ from a self-chosen app

This is the finding with the largest design consequences, and it is well evidenced from two directions.

**Direction 1 — mode and disclosure.** Adolescents disclose more to a screen than to a person. In an Australian study of survey administration mode ([PMC10559918](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10559918/)), interviewer-administered surveys returned **~10 percentage points lower "very high distress"** than online self-report (9.9% vs 19.0% in 2020). The effect sizes were statistically weak (η² < 0.03) but the authors' point stands: *"even a weak effect can have a large impact on mental health prevalence rates."* Notably, the mode effect was pronounced for **18–25s** and minimal for 12–17s — so for this app's 16–18 cohort the effect sits right on the boundary and should be assumed to matter. The practical implication is favourable: **a digital, self-administered check-in is the right instrument.** The app already has that advantage; it just has to avoid throwing it away.

**Direction 2 — consequence, and this is where an academy differs from Kooth.** A young person choosing Kooth is anonymous and the service holds no power over them. A student answering this app's survey is named, on a college roster, at a football academy, and the answers are visible to the coaches who pick teams. That is a different instrument even with identical questions.

The academy literature is unambiguous about what follows:

- Players report *"living in fear that any disclosure of personal information will be transmitted to others within the organisation"*, which the authors describe as engendering *"a culture of mistrust within the academy system"* ([exploring the mental health and wellbeing of professional academy footballers in England](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14660970.2021.1952693)).
- The stepped-care review of academy MH provision names the top drawback of in-house clinicians as *"players' fear that their MH disclosures may not remain confidential, potentially affecting team selection or future placement at the club"*, and notes external provision *"provides greater perceived confidentiality"* ([PMC12355608](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12355608/)). The Burnley FC Academy pilot of that framework flagged *"maintaining players' confidentiality within a data-hungry sporting environment"* as one of four critical implementation lessons.
- Athletes distinguish sharply between *who* holds the data. From the 11-athlete study: *"I'll be honest with my national team coach because I trust him, but I'll ask if he is giving the data to [name] in my club team."* Visibility to "contract-focused" staff, as opposed to athlete-focused medical staff, measurably reduced honesty.
- And the reasons for withholding are specific and recognisable in a 16-year-old: not wanting to appear *"unprofessional"* or *"lacking motivation"*; fear of being pulled from training; *"I ask myself how honest should I be?… how is this going to be used?"*

The counterweight is equally specific. Athletes said they told the truth when *"[they] knew he was in it for us and would use the information to make us better"*, when *"my information is acted upon"*, and when they could see *"our workouts are adjusted based on our feedback."* The study's own conclusion is the sharpest line in this document: **it challenges the assumption that subjective monitoring fails because athletes are dishonest, and concludes instead that staff behaviours create the conditions that produce dishonesty.**

**Four design rules follow for an academy-mandated check-in that do not apply to a consumer app:**

| Rule | Why it applies here and not to Kooth |
|---|---|
| **State who sees each field, on the screen where it is answered.** | The student cannot opt out of the relationship, so ambiguity is coercive rather than merely unclear. Uncertainty alone suppresses honest answers — informed consent, per the researchers. |
| **Firewall wellbeing data from selection.** Scores must not be visible to staff who pick teams, and the app should say so explicitly. | Deselection fear is the single named barrier in the academy literature. Right now every coach can see every score. |
| **Give the student a channel with real distance from the coaching staff** — the equivalent of Kooth's anonymity or the literature's "external clinician". | A named-and-visible-to-your-coach channel will never carry the disclosures that matter most; a second channel is not a duplicate, it is the one that works. |
| **Never make honesty cost something.** No "flagged" label the student can see, no visible consequence for a low score, and never a punishment or chase for non-completion. | Punishment-based enforcement backfires by reducing honest reporting (Saw/Main/Gastin). A student who learns that a 2 triggers an awkward conversation will answer 4 forever. |

One nuance worth naming: **full anonymity is not available to this app and should not be pretended.** Safeguarding requires attribution — an anonymous red flag cannot be acted on. youHQ's honest framing is the model: it claims anonymity to *reduce pressure*, in the context of a system that still surfaces individual patterns to staff. The right posture is **explicit confidentiality with a named, narrow audience**, not implied anonymity that a student will eventually discover is false. Discovering that is worse than never having claimed it.

## 5. Prioritized recommendations for this app

Tied to `SURVEY_QUESTIONS` in `lib/wellbeing/wellbeingUtils.ts` and the student page at `app/(student)/wellbeing/page.tsx`. Ordered by value per unit of effort. Items 1–3 are the ones that change data validity; everything below is improvement rather than repair.

**1. Show the student their own trend on the completion screen. (S)**
Replace *"Your responses have been saved. See you next week."* with the student's own last 4–6 check-ins. This is the single highest-value change in this document because it is the only one that attacks the write-only defect directly, and youHQ leads its product on it. It also fixes an incidental bug: the copy says "next week" and "will arrive on a Monday" for a **fortnightly** survey.
*Why:* the athlete literature is explicit that invisible loops produce dummy responses.

**2. Add a "who sees this" line to the survey and the note field, then make it true. (S, plus the access fix)**
One sentence above the questions naming exactly who reads the answers, and a second one on the note field. Do this **at the same time** as locking `/admin/wellbeing` to the DSL — writing a confidentiality promise the code does not keep is worse than writing nothing.
*Why:* uncertainty about data access suppresses honest reporting on its own, and both CPOMS and MyConcern treat role-scoped access as baseline.

**3. Fix `stress` by adopting WHO-5 rather than patching the reverse-coding. (M)**
Last night's doc correctly identifies the reverse-coding defect as the top fix. The cheapest *durable* version of that fix is to replace the home-grown items with **WHO-5**: five items, all positively worded so the bug class cannot recur, 0–5 frequency scale, a two-week window that matches the fortnightly cadence exactly, a published cut-off (<50) with sensitivity 89% / specificity 86%, and adolescent validation. Keep **`football_enjoyment`** alongside it as a sixth, academy-specific item — it is the item that makes the survey feel like it belongs to them, and buy-in drives honest completion.
*Why:* it removes the scale-direction defect permanently, gives a defensible threshold instead of the current arbitrary `<= 2`, and is already in production in a UK college wellbeing product.
*Caveat:* WHO-5 measures a two-week window, so it cannot double as a daily-state measure. That is what item 4 is for. Also treat pre-change data as a separate series — do not backfill.

**4. Add a one-tap mood check on the dashboard, always available, separate from the survey. (M)**
youHQ's split. One emoji tap at login or from the dashboard, with **optional** cascading follow-ups in youHQ's phrasing — *"What's behind that feeling?"*, *"Has it changed since yesterday?"* — and a Kooth-style route: tapping a low feeling offers a next step (message a named tutor, a stem4 tool, the AI Coach) rather than just recording a number.
*Why:* it gives the continuous signal the fortnightly survey cannot, gives students a way to check in between surveys (currently impossible), and — importantly — it does this **without increasing mandated questioning**, which the Spielman/EEF evidence says to avoid. It is pull, not push. **It must add zero notifications**; last night's engagement doc found the notification budget already overspent at 6–9/day.

**5. Route a red flag into `safeguarding_concerns` with an owner, and stop the all-staff broadcast. (M)**
Bounce Together syncs concerning answers to CPOMS; MyConcern auto-notifies the DSL. This app pushes a named student's distress to every admin, coach and teacher. Notify the DSL, create the record, keep the audit trail.
*Why:* it is both the safeguarding-record requirement from last night's doc and, per §4, a direct fix to the deselection-fear problem — the coaching staff being told is the thing the student is afraid of.

**6. Render the free-text note to the DSL. (S)**
Already Tier 0 in last night's doc; restating only because this survey confirms it is the difference between a working system and a broken one. A note is the highest-signal field and is currently displayed to nobody.

**7. Move to individual 28-day z-score baselines with a ±1.5 SD flag, and treat a flag as a prompt for a conversation, not an action. (M)**
Published practitioner convention, and it corroborates the individual-baseline recommendation already made. The phrasing matters: *"significant deviations… should trigger a conversation rather than immediate action."*

**8. Correlate wellbeing against data the app already holds. (M)**
Daylio's mechanic. Attendance, GPS load, sessions and fixtures are already stored. "Your sleep scores drop the week after an away fixture" is genuinely useful to a 17-year-old and costs no new collection. Show it to the student first, staff second.

**9. Co-design the final question set with the students. (S, but calendar time)**
#BeeWell co-created its survey with young people at ~200,000-participant scale; MindMate was co-produced with young people. There are ~66 students and a working chat system. Run it before item 3 ships if the timing allows.

**Explicitly recommend against:**
- **Making the survey weekly or daily.** The 153-school / 12,166-pupil trial and STEER's cadence analysis both point the other way. Fortnightly for the mandated instrument is defensible; add the pull-based channel in item 4 instead.
- **Any imposed streak, badge, leaderboard or composite "wellness score."** If a streak is wanted at all, it must be Finch-shaped: opt-in, chosen duration, zero penalty for missing.
- **Claiming anonymity.** Safeguarding requires attribution. Promise narrow, named confidentiality and honour it.
- **Chasing non-completion with escalating notifications.** Non-response is the strongest withdrawal signal (last night's doc) and should trigger a *human* checking in, not the app nagging — punishment-based enforcement reduces honest reporting.

## 6. Open questions for Paul

1. **Who is the DSL, and are they distinct from the coaching staff?** Every recommendation in §4 and §5 depends on wellbeing data having a named owner who does not pick teams. If the DSL *is* a coach, the firewall recommendation needs rethinking — the alternative would be an external route (a Kooth or Tellmi signpost) doing that job instead.
2. **Do you want to keep home-grown questions, or adopt WHO-5?** WHO-5 buys a defensible threshold and permanently removes the reverse-coding bug class, but it costs the football-specific texture and makes the historical series non-comparable. Keeping `football_enjoyment` alongside is my recommendation; confirm that's acceptable.
3. **Is a second, lower-visibility channel viable here?** The literature says the disclosures that matter will not come through a named, coach-visible survey. Options: signpost Kooth/Tellmi explicitly; make the AI Coach that channel with a proper safeguarding prompt and a DSL-only escalation queue; or accept the gap. This is a policy decision, not a technical one.
4. **What does the college's existing safeguarding policy say about wellbeing data retention and access?** CPOMS-class products build the retention and access rules in. If the college already runs CPOMS or MyConcern alongside this app, the right answer may be to route flags *into* that system rather than duplicate it — worth checking before building item 5.
5. **Are parents/carers in scope at all?** Nothing in this app touches them, and the EPPP/academy literature assumes parental involvement in player care. Deliberate omission or gap?
6. **How much appetite is there for student co-design before shipping?** Item 9 is cheap and high-credibility but adds calendar time. Worth it if the question set is being changed anyway.
7. **Should the login-time mood check be visible to staff at all, or student-private?** youHQ surfaces it to a teacher dashboard. Keeping it student-private would make it a genuinely safe space and a better honest-signal source, at the cost of the staff-facing continuous signal. Both are defensible; the choice should be explicit.
